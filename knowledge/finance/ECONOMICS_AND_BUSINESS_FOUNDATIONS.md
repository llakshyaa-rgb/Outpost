# Economics and Business Foundations

**Coverage:** introductory global guide. Tax, accounting standards, banking products and business law vary by jurisdiction.

**Search terms:** economics, microeconomics, macroeconomics, inflation, GDP, trade, business, entrepreneurship, accounting, taxation, banking, money, markets, supply and demand.

## Economics
Economics studies choices under scarcity and how resources are produced, distributed and consumed. Microeconomics examines households, firms, incentives, prices and market structures. Macroeconomics studies output, employment, inflation, interest rates, public budgets and economic growth. Models simplify reality and depend on assumptions.

## Supply, demand and markets
Demand describes quantities buyers are willing and able to purchase at different prices; supply describes quantities sellers are willing and able to offer. Equilibrium is where planned supply and demand meet in a simplified model. Real markets are affected by market power, information, regulation, externalities, public goods, expectations and institutions.

## National accounts and indicators
Gross domestic product (GDP) measures the value of final goods and services produced within a territory over a period. Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP adjusts for price changes. GDP per capita is an average, not a measure of how income is distributed or of overall wellbeing. Inflation measures changes in a price index; unemployment definitions and labour-force participation matter when comparing countries.

## Money and policy
Central banks influence monetary conditions through policy rates and other tools. Governments use taxation, spending and borrowing as fiscal policy. Exchange rates reflect multiple forces, including interest rates, trade, capital flows and expectations. Institutions and policy frameworks differ by country.

## Business foundations
A business model explains the value offered, customers, delivery method, revenue and costs. Common functions include operations, marketing, sales, finance, accounting, human resources, product development, compliance and customer support. Cash flow, profitability and growth are different measures. Revenue is not profit; profit is not cash on hand.

## Accounting literacy
The income statement reports performance over a period; the balance sheet reports assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time; the cash-flow statement tracks cash movements. Accounting standards differ, including IFRS and national GAAP frameworks. Financial statements require notes and context.

## International commerce
Trade involves goods, services, investment, intellectual property and supply chains. Tariffs, quotas, standards, logistics, exchange rates and geopolitical risks affect trade. Global value chains can cross many jurisdictions and create both efficiencies and vulnerabilities.
