# Personal Finance, Economics and Accounting — Field Guide


**Search terms:** personal finance, budgeting, saving, debt, credit, banking, accounting, bookkeeping, balance sheet, income statement, cash flow, taxes.

## Personal finance foundations
Track income, fixed costs, variable spending, debt payments, savings and irregular expenses. A budget is a plan, not a judgement. Build an emergency reserve appropriate to income stability and essential expenses. Keep short-term money in instruments whose risk and access match the time it is needed. Understand deposit protection limits and exclusions in the relevant country.

### Interest and debt
Simple interest is calculated on principal. Compound interest adds earned interest to the balance, so future interest can accrue on prior interest. Effective annual rates help compare compounding periods. For borrowing, compare total repayment, fees, penalties, variable-rate clauses and currency risk—not just the advertised rate. High-cost revolving debt can compound quickly.

### Inflation and purchasing power
Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. Approximate real return = (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) − 1. A positive nominal return can still lose purchasing power after inflation, fees and taxes.

### Insurance and risk transfer
Insurance transfers specified risks in exchange for premiums, subject to exclusions, deductibles, limits and claims conditions. Compare coverage, insurer solvency, exclusions and renewal terms. Insurance is not an investment substitute by default.

## Accounting fundamentals
The accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Double-entry bookkeeping records equal debits and credits. Revenue recognition, accruals, depreciation, inventory valuation, provisions and impairment affect reported results. Cash accounting and accrual accounting answer different questions.

### Key ratios
- Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities.
- Debt-to-equity = total debt / equity (definitions vary).
- Gross margin = (revenue − cost of goods sold) / revenue.
- Operating margin = operating income / revenue.
- Return on equity = net income / average equity.
Ratios require context, consistent definitions and comparison with industry peers.

## Economics
Microeconomics studies choices by households and firms, supply and demand, competition, incentives, market failure and externalities. Macroeconomics studies output, employment, inflation, interest rates, fiscal policy, monetary policy, exchange rates and trade. GDP measures production, not total wellbeing. Nominal values are not adjusted for inflation; real values are.

### Core terms
Opportunity cost, scarcity, marginal utility, elasticity, productivity, comparative advantage, business cycle, unemployment, CPI, PPI, policy rate, yield curve, current account, fiscal deficit and public debt.

## Business and entrepreneurship
Understand customer problems, willingness to pay, market size, distribution, unit economics, gross margin, contribution margin, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, churn, working capital, cash conversion cycle, inventory risk and break-even point. Revenue growth does not guarantee profitability or cash survival.

## Data and decision hygiene
Use original records where possible. Keep units, currencies, time periods and definitions consistent. Distinguish correlation from causation. Record assumptions, uncertainty and source dates. Avoid false precision in forecasts.
