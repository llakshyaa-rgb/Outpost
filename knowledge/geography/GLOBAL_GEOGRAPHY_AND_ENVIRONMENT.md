# Global Geography and Environment

**Coverage:** introductory guide. Climate records, protected-area status, population figures and environmental rules change; consult dated authoritative datasets for current figures.

**Search terms:** geography, world geography, countries, continents, maps, climate, weather, environment, ecology, ecosystem, biodiversity, pollution, conservation, natural resources, disasters, sustainability.

## Physical geography
Earth's surface includes continents, islands, ocean basins, mountains, plateaus, plains, deserts, forests, rivers, lakes, glaciers and wetlands. Plate tectonics explains much of the distribution of earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain belts and ocean trenches. The water cycle moves water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration and runoff.

## Human geography
Human geography studies population, settlement, cities, migration, languages, culture, economic activity, borders and the relationships between people and place. Country borders and political status can be disputed; maps should state their source, date and conventions rather than imply every boundary is universally agreed.

## Weather and climate
Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions; climate describes long-term patterns and variability. Climate is shaped by latitude, altitude, oceans, circulation, land cover and greenhouse gases. Climate change affects heat, precipitation, sea level, ecosystems, agriculture and risks to communities, with impacts varying by region.

## Ecology and biodiversity
An ecosystem includes organisms and their physical environment. Energy flows through food webs while nutrients cycle. Biodiversity exists at genetic, species and ecosystem levels. Habitat loss, invasive species, pollution, overexploitation and climate change are major pressures. Conservation approaches include protected areas, restoration, sustainable management and community-led stewardship.

## Pollution and resources
Air, water, soil, noise, light and plastic pollution can affect human and ecosystem health. Resource topics include freshwater, forests, fisheries, minerals, fossil fuels and renewable energy. Assess full life-cycle impacts, distributional fairness and the rights of affected communities.

## Natural hazards and preparedness
Hazards include earthquakes, tsunamis, cyclones, floods, droughts, landslides, wildfires, heatwaves and volcanic eruptions. Risk depends on hazard, exposure and vulnerability. Follow local early-warning systems, evacuation instructions and official emergency agencies; hazard maps and building standards are region-specific.

## Global data sources
For current information, consult national meteorological and geological agencies, national statistics offices, the World Meteorological Organization, World Health Organization for health impacts, UN environment bodies, IPCC assessment reports and recognised scientific datasets. Check definitions, collection dates, geographic coverage and uncertainty before comparing numbers.
