# World History and Archaeology

**Coverage:** introductory global framework; regional depth is still needed.

**Search terms:** history, world history, civilisation, civilization, archaeology, ancient history, prehistory, empire, colonialism, decolonisation, world wars, historical sources.

## Historical method
History studies change over time using written records, oral histories, archaeology, objects, architecture, environmental evidence and scientific analysis. A source reflects its creator, purpose, audience and context. Historians compare sources, consider missing voices and distinguish evidence from interpretation. Dates and period labels can vary across regions.

## Human origins and early societies
Homo sapiens evolved in Africa and spread through multiple migrations. Foraging communities developed diverse technologies, social structures, languages and artistic traditions. Agriculture emerged independently in several regions, supporting permanent settlements and population growth, but also changing labour, health, property and political organisation.

## Early urban societies
Complex societies developed in Mesopotamia, the Nile Valley, the Indus region, China, the Americas and elsewhere. They created varied systems of writing, trade, irrigation, law, monumental building and administration. Avoid treating any single region as the sole origin of civilisation; archaeological evidence continues to change interpretations.

## Connected ancient and post-classical worlds
Trade networks across Africa, Asia, Europe and the Indian and Pacific oceans moved goods, technologies, religions, people and disease. Empires, city-states, kingdoms, confederations and Indigenous polities developed different forms of authority. The word “medieval” is a regional period label, not a universal stage of human history.

## Early modern to contemporary history
Oceanic navigation expanded global connections through trade, migration, conquest and colonisation. Enslavement and forced labour shaped many economies. Industrialisation transformed energy, manufacturing, transport and urban life. Revolutions, nationalism, imperialism and anti-colonial movements reshaped states. The world wars, genocides, Cold War, decolonisation and post-1945 institutions transformed international relations. Contemporary history remains contested and new evidence may emerge.

## Archaeological methods
Archaeologists study context, stratigraphy, artefacts, buildings, landscapes and human remains. Dating methods include radiocarbon dating for suitable organic material and other techniques for different materials and time ranges. Context is essential: removing an object without recording its location destroys evidence. Cultural heritage work should respect descendant and Indigenous communities.

## Historical source checklist
Ask who created a source, when, for what purpose, whose viewpoint it represents, what evidence corroborates it, what is missing and how certain the interpretation is. Be cautious with nationalist myths, fabricated quotations, anachronistic labels and simplified timelines.

## Known gaps
