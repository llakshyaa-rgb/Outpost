# Law and Government: A Global Overview

**Coverage:** introductory comparative guide; not legal advice. Laws are jurisdiction-specific and change. Never infer that a rule in one country applies elsewhere.

**Search terms:** law, legal system, legislation, statute, regulation, constitution, rights, government, courts, criminal law, civil law, contract, property, family law, labour law, immigration, privacy, international law, legal rights.

## Sources and systems of law
Legal systems draw on different combinations of constitutions, legislation, regulations, judicial decisions, customary law and religious law. Common-law systems give precedent a prominent role; civil-law systems generally organise law through codes and statutes; mixed systems combine traditions. These labels are broad and do not determine every rule. The legal hierarchy and court structure must be checked country by country.

## Government and public institutions
Many states divide authority among legislative, executive and judicial institutions, but constitutional arrangements vary. Some are federal; others are unitary or devolved. Elections, parties, public agencies, local government and independent institutions operate under each country's constitutional framework. A constitution may define state powers, rights and amendment procedures.

## Main legal fields
- **Criminal law:** offences, investigation, prosecution, trial, sentencing and safeguards.
- **Civil law:** disputes between people or organisations, compensation and remedies.
- **Contract law:** formation, interpretation, performance, breach and remedies.
- **Property law:** ownership, possession, land, intellectual property and transfer.
- **Family law:** marriage, separation, parenting, adoption and inheritance.
- **Employment law:** wages, hours, safety, discrimination, dismissal and collective rights.
- **Consumer law:** product safety, misleading claims, refunds, warranties and unfair terms.
- **Public and administrative law:** government powers, decisions, review and accountability.
- **Privacy and data protection:** collection, use, security and rights relating to personal information.
- **Immigration law:** entry, residence, work, asylum, citizenship and removal.

## Rights and due process
Human-rights instruments establish international standards, but implementation and remedies depend on treaties, domestic law and institutions. Rights may include equality, expression, religion or belief, privacy, fair trial, education and protection from torture. Rights can have lawful limitations; the scope and enforcement differ. Due process generally concerns fair procedures, access to counsel, impartial decision-makers and the presumption of innocence in criminal proceedings.

## International law
International law includes treaties, customary international law and general principles. It governs relations among states and, in some areas, organisations and individuals. The United Nations system, international courts and tribunals, trade institutions and regional organisations have distinct mandates. A treaty's effect inside a country depends on that country's constitutional approach to international obligations.

## How to research a current legal question
1. Identify the exact country and, where relevant, state, province, territory or municipality.
2. Identify the legal issue and date when the relevant conduct occurred.
3. Find the official gazette, legislation database, court or government source.
4. Check amendments, commencement dates, regulations and binding judgments.
5. Distinguish binding law from guidance, commentary and news reports.
6. For a consequential personal matter, consult a qualified lawyer or legal-aid service in that jurisdiction.
