# Research, Data, Units and Source Literacy

**Coverage:** practical reference framework. Specific standards and datasets should be checked against their current issuing bodies.

**Search terms:** research, data, statistics, units, conversion, measurement, SI, source evaluation, evidence, citation, dataset, uncertainty, reproducibility.

## Measurement and units
The International System of Units (SI) defines seven base units: metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela. Derived units combine these. Some countries and industries also use customary units. Always state units, conversion assumptions, significant figures and measurement uncertainty. A number without a unit or reference date may be misleading.

## Research process
Define a question, identify relevant prior work, select methods, collect data ethically, document procedures, analyse results and report limitations. Pre-registration can reduce selective reporting in some fields. Reproducibility means methods and data allow a result to be checked; replication tests whether findings recur in new work.

## Data literacy
Understand what each row and column represents, how values were collected, missing-data rules, sample selection, definitions and update frequency. Distinguish counts, rates, percentages, averages, medians and index values. Beware of misleading axes, truncated scales, cherry-picked time periods, survivorship bias, confounding and correlation-causation errors.

## Evaluating sources
Prefer primary documents for exact legal or technical requirements, official statistics for government data, systematic reviews for broad research synthesis and recognised professional bodies for standards. Check authorship, expertise, methods, date, citations, conflicts of interest, geographic coverage and corrections. Official sources can be incomplete; independent corroboration is valuable.

## Citation and provenance
Record title, author or organisation, publication date, version, URL or archive location, access date and relevant page or section. Preserve dataset metadata and licence terms. Do not remove attribution or imply a source supports a claim it does not make.

## Global reference data
Population, GDP, exchange rates, climate records, disease statistics, internet use and other indicators depend on definitions and collection methods. Compare like with like: year, currency basis, purchasing-power adjustment, geographic boundary, population denominator and uncertainty. Dynamic values must be date-stamped and should not be treated as live when stored offline.

## Known gaps
Detailed conversion tables, physical constants, language and country codes, calendars, time zones, statistical methods, data formats and discipline-specific standards need dedicated reference pages.
