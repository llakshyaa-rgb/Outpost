# Mathematics and the Natural Sciences

**Coverage:** broad introductory survey, not specialist textbooks.

**Search terms:** mathematics, maths, math, physics, chemistry, biology, natural science, statistics, probability, algebra, geometry, mechanics, atoms, cells, evolution.

## Mathematics
Mathematics studies quantity, structure, space, change and uncertainty. Arithmetic handles numbers and operations; algebra represents relationships with symbols; geometry studies shapes and space; trigonometry relates angles and lengths; calculus studies change and accumulation; linear algebra studies vectors and transformations; discrete mathematics studies countable structures, logic and algorithms. Probability models uncertainty. Statistics describes data and makes inferences while accounting for sampling error, bias and uncertainty. Mathematical models simplify reality and must be checked against observations.

## Physics
Physics investigates matter, energy, motion and interactions. Classical mechanics describes motion and forces; thermodynamics studies heat, work and energy; fluid mechanics studies liquids and gases; waves and acoustics describe oscillations and sound; optics studies light; electromagnetism describes electric and magnetic fields; quantum mechanics describes microscopic systems; relativity concerns space, time and gravity. Models apply within assumptions and domains; Newtonian mechanics remains useful at everyday speeds even though relativity is more general.

## Chemistry
Chemistry studies matter, its composition, structure, properties and transformations. Atoms contain nuclei and electrons; chemical bonds form through interactions between atoms. Reactions rearrange atoms and conserve total atoms and charge. Major areas include organic, inorganic, physical, analytical and biochemistry. Concentration, temperature, pressure, catalysts and reaction conditions influence rates and equilibria. Chemical hazard depends on substance, dose, route and exposure; never mix unknown household chemicals.

## Biology
Biology studies living systems. Cells are basic units of life; DNA stores hereditary information and genes influence traits through expression and interaction with environments. Evolution by natural selection, mutation, genetic drift and gene flow explains changes in populations over generations. Microbiology studies microorganisms; botany plants; zoology animals; ecology relationships among organisms and environments; physiology function; anatomy structure. Biological categories are useful models but real variation can be complex.

## Earth and environmental sciences
Geology studies Earth's materials, structure and history. Plate tectonics explains many earthquakes, volcanoes and mountain belts. Meteorology studies weather; climatology long-term climate; oceanography marine systems; hydrology water movement; paleontology ancient life. Environmental science integrates physical, biological and social knowledge to understand pollution, resource use, climate and conservation.

## Scientific method and uncertainty
A scientific investigation defines a question, develops testable hypotheses, gathers observations, analyses data and reports methods and limitations. Correlation alone does not prove causation. Replication, peer criticism, transparent methods and converging evidence strengthen confidence. Measurements have uncertainty; significant digits should not imply false precision.
