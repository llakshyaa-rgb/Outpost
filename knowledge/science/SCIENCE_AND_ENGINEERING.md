# Science, Mathematics and Engineering — Field Guide


**Search terms:** science, engineering, physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics, technology, scientific method, engineering disciplines.

## Scientific method
State a testable question, review prior evidence, define hypotheses, design a controlled method, collect observations, analyse uncertainty and publish reproducible methods. A hypothesis is not confirmed merely because one observation agrees with it. Correlation alone does not establish causation. Replication and independent evidence matter.

## Mathematics
Arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, calculus, probability, statistics, linear algebra and discrete mathematics form a shared toolkit. Track units and significant figures. A model is a simplified representation; state assumptions and limits. Probability describes uncertainty, not certainty about an individual outcome.

## Physics
Mechanics studies motion and forces; thermodynamics studies heat, work and energy; electromagnetism studies electric and magnetic fields; optics studies light; quantum physics and relativity describe domains where classical models are insufficient. Conservation laws are powerful checks on calculations.

## Chemistry and materials
Atoms, molecules, bonding, reactions, stoichiometry, equilibrium, acids and bases, redox, kinetics and thermochemistry explain chemical behaviour. Safety data sheets, ventilation, protective equipment and incompatibilities matter. Never mix unknown chemicals.

## Biology and ecology
Cells, genetics, evolution, physiology, microbiology and ecosystems connect life at different scales. Natural selection acts on heritable variation in populations. Ecosystems exchange energy and cycle matter; biodiversity supports resilience but does not guarantee immunity from disturbance.

## Earth and climate
Earth systems include atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere and biosphere. Weather is short-term atmospheric behaviour; climate describes long-term statistical patterns. Climate attribution uses multiple lines of evidence and physical mechanisms. Natural hazards include earthquakes, volcanoes, floods, storms, drought and landslides.

## Engineering method
Translate needs into requirements, constraints and measurable acceptance criteria. Develop concepts, model loads and failure modes, prototype, test, document and maintain. Include safety factors, tolerances, reliability, lifecycle cost, accessibility and environmental impact. Follow applicable codes and qualified professional review for safety-critical structures and systems.

## Energy and electricity
Power is the rate of energy transfer; energy is power integrated over time. Voltage, current, resistance and power are related by circuit laws. Batteries have chemistry-specific voltage, capacity, charge limits and hazards. Grid systems balance generation, demand, storage and transmission; energy sources have different cost, reliability and environmental profiles.
