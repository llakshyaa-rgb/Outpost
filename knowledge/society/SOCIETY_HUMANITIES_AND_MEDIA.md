# Society, Humanities and Media Literacy

**Coverage:** introductory guide; societies are diverse and no single cultural description applies to every person in a region.

**Search terms:** society, sociology, psychology, anthropology, philosophy, ethics, culture, politics, education, language, linguistics, media literacy, misinformation, propaganda, communication.

## Psychology
Psychology studies behaviour and mental processes, including perception, memory, learning, emotion, development, motivation and social interaction. Findings depend on methods and samples; effects observed in one population may not generalise universally. Mental-health conditions require careful assessment, not online labels.

## Sociology and anthropology
Sociology examines institutions, groups, inequality, norms, organisations and social change. Anthropology studies humans across cultural, biological, linguistic and archaeological perspectives. Concepts such as family, class, identity, religion and community vary across time and place; avoid treating a culture as uniform.

## Philosophy and ethics
Philosophy examines knowledge, reality, reasoning, values, mind, language and political organisation. Logic studies valid inference. Ethics considers what people ought to do, including consequences, duties, rights, virtues and care. Real-world dilemmas can involve competing values rather than one universally accepted answer.

## Education and language
Education includes formal schooling, vocational learning, informal learning and lifelong development. Linguistics studies language structure, meaning, sound, change and use. Languages have dialects and varieties; no dialect is inherently less logical or valuable. Translation can lose nuance and context.

## Politics and civic life
Political science studies power, institutions, public policy, political behaviour and international relations. Political systems vary; descriptive explanations should be distinguished from political advocacy. Evaluate claims using evidence and identify whose interests or experiences are missing.

## Media and information literacy
- Identify the original publisher, author, date and evidence.
- Distinguish reporting, opinion, advertising, satire and sponsored content.
- Trace images and quotations to their original context.
- Check whether multiple independent, credible sources corroborate a claim.
- Watch for emotional manipulation, false dilemmas, cherry-picked statistics and fabricated authority.
- Understand that recommendation algorithms can amplify attention-grabbing material.
- Correct errors transparently and update conclusions when evidence changes.

## Research and source quality
Primary sources provide direct records or data but still need interpretation. Secondary sources analyse primary material. Tertiary sources summarise established knowledge. Evaluate expertise, methods, conflicts of interest, reproducibility, citations, date and scope. A prestigious source can still make mistakes; a viral post is not evidence by itself.
