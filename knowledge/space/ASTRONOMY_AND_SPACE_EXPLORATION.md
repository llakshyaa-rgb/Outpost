# Astronomy and Space Exploration

**Coverage:** introductory guide. Observational discoveries, mission status and launch schedules change; use dated sources.

**Search terms:** astronomy, space, universe, planets, stars, galaxies, astrophysics, cosmology, solar system, satellite, rocket, spacecraft, spaceflight, orbit.

## Observing the sky
Astronomy studies celestial objects and phenomena using visible light and many other parts of the electromagnetic spectrum, as well as neutrinos, cosmic rays and gravitational waves. Telescopes collect signals; instruments measure them. Observations require calibration and interpretation, and light travel time means distant objects are seen as they were in the past.

## Solar system
The Sun is a star. Planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets and smaller bodies orbit under gravity. Planetary science studies surfaces, interiors, atmospheres, magnetic fields and histories. Earth is one planet among many; evidence for life beyond Earth has not yet established confirmed extraterrestrial life.

## Stars and galaxies
Stars form from collapsing gas, generate energy through nuclear fusion and evolve according to mass and composition. Galaxies contain stars, gas, dust and dark matter. Black holes are regions where gravity is so strong that within the event horizon escape is impossible. Cosmology studies the universe's large-scale history and expansion.

## Orbits and spacecraft
An orbit is continuous free-fall around a body. Orbital speed and period depend on gravitational parameters and orbital shape. Satellites support communications, navigation, weather observation, Earth science and research. Spacecraft require power, thermal control, communications, propulsion, guidance and protection from radiation and debris.

## Launch and exploration
Launch vehicles deliver payloads to trajectories; missions may use flybys, orbiters, landers, rovers or sample return. Human spaceflight adds life support, radiation exposure, microgravity, medical and operational challenges. Planetary protection seeks to avoid harmful biological contamination in both directions.

## Space law and sustainability
Space activities are governed by international treaties and national licensing regimes. Issues include registration, liability, spectrum use, resource claims, debris mitigation and responsible behaviour. Space is not free of environmental or geopolitical concerns.

## Known gaps
