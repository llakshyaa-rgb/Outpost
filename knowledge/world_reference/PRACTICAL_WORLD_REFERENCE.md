# Practical World Reference — Health, Geography, History and Everyday Skills


**Search terms:** world reference, countries, capitals, currencies, languages, time zones, geography, units, global facts, reference data.

## Health literacy
Use reliable clinical and public-health sources. Learn basic anatomy, infection prevention, nutrition, sleep, mental wellbeing and how to recognise emergencies. First aid is time-sensitive: call local emergency services, follow dispatcher instructions and use training appropriate to the situation. This guide does not diagnose or replace medical care. Drug names, doses and availability vary by country and patient.

## Emergency readiness
Know local emergency numbers, evacuation routes, hazards and official alerts. Keep water, essential medicines, basic first-aid supplies, light, communication power, copies of important documents and a family contact plan appropriate to local risks. Do not enter floodwater, damaged structures or hazardous areas without training and official clearance.

## Geography
Learn continents, oceans, countries, capitals, major rivers, mountain systems, biomes, climate zones, time zones and map projections. Borders and place names can be disputed or change; record the map's date and source. Coordinates and map projections are not interchangeable.

## World history
Study chronology alongside geography, primary sources, archaeology, economic systems, migration, technology, institutions and cultural exchange. Historical accounts reflect the evidence available and the perspective of their authors. Compare sources and distinguish evidence from interpretation.

## Law and government worldwide
Legal systems include common-law, civil-law, customary, religious and mixed traditions. Government structures and rights protections vary. For a legal question, identify the country and subnational jurisdiction, relevant date, court or authority, and whether the source is binding primary law or explanatory commentary. Check official gazettes, legislation portals and court sites. International treaties bind states according to their terms and participation; they do not automatically operate identically in every domestic legal system. This reference is educational, not legal advice.

## Agriculture and food
Soil health, water management, crop rotation, plant nutrition, animal welfare, food safety, storage temperatures and contamination prevention affect yields and health. Agricultural recommendations are climate-, soil-, species- and region-dependent. Consult local agricultural extension and food-safety authorities.

## Environment and sustainability
Consider resource extraction, land use, biodiversity, pollution, waste, water, energy and lifecycle impacts. Recycling is only one part of material management; reduction, repair, reuse and safe disposal also matter.

## Languages and communication
Language learning combines listening, speaking, reading, writing, vocabulary, grammar and cultural context. Translation is rarely word-for-word; preserve meaning, register and audience. For high-stakes legal, medical or technical communication, use qualified translators.

## Learning and research skills
Define a question, search for primary sources, evaluate authorship and evidence, compare independent sources, note publication dates and keep citations. Distinguish peer-reviewed research, preprints, official statistics, journalism, opinion and advertising. A source can be authoritative in one domain and weak in another.
